    Tracks¶

    All data is generated from seeds by the package itself, so there is nothing to download and no licensed data is involved. Every metric is higher-is-better except FPR and residual leakage.

    Each track has its own benchmark version (TRACKS[key].version), so changing one track's suite only marks that track's results stale:

    Track Version Changes
    embedding-detection 1.1 30 null datasets per variant instead of 10
    embedding-mitigation 1.0
    image-detection 1.0
    image-mitigation 1.0

    Ranking the detection tracks¶

    A detection entry returns a continuous score and, optionally, a yes/no flag. The score is ranked by AUROC, which says nothing about the flag: a detector whose flag fires on every dataset can still order its scores well. So on both detection tracks the table is ordered in three groups, each by AUROC:

    1. entries whose flag fires on at most 20% of null datasets (shortcut_detect.leaderboard.MAX_FLAG_FPR),
    2. entries that return no flag,
    3. entries whose flag fires on more than 20% of null datasets, marked flag FPR > 0.2.

    A flag at a nominal α = 0.05 stays well under the cap. Leaving the flag out cannot lift an entry above one whose flag is calibrated.

    embedding-detection¶

    Question. Do embeddings E carry an attribute A beyond what the task label Y explains?

    Data. shortcut_detect.benchmark.conditional_synthetic.generate_conditional_synthetic with n = 600, 64 dimensions, A agreeing with Y 70% of the time, 4 task dimensions and 4 attribute dimensions, columns shuffled per dataset. The attribute signal has strength δ ∈ {0 (null), 0.3, 0.6, 1.0} and one of three forms:

    Variant How A is carried What it rewards
    linear mean shift of δ standard deviations the easy case
    xor sign of the product of dimension pairs; no mean difference non-linear detectors
    variance equal means, standard deviation 1 + δ in one group detectors beyond the mean

    Null datasets (δ = 0) still have A correlated with Y, so E predicts A through the task dimensions. A method that ignores Y fires on them.

    Suite. Per variant, 10 seeds for each of the three strengths δ ∈ {0.3, 0.6, 1.0} and 30 null seeds (δ = 0), so nulls and shortcut datasets are equal in number: 3 × 60 = 180 datasets (quick: 2 seeds each). Version 1.0 had 10 nulls per variant; with that few, a per-variant AUROC moved by about ±0.15 from seed noise alone. Even with 30, a method that cannot see a variant scores 0.5 ± 0.075 (one standard deviation) on it, so read a per-variant AUROC well under 0.5 as "blind to this variant", not as worse than chance. For example, the conditional probe is linear and cannot see the XOR signal: it scores 0.41 on the public XOR seeds (0.31 under version 1.0) and 0.55–0.59 on four sets of fresh seeds.

    Entry. detect(embeddings, attribute, task_labels, seed) -> float | {"score", "flag"}.

    Metrics. auroc: how well the score separates shortcut from null datasets, averaged over the three variants (ranking metric, after the flag groups); per-variant AUROC. With a flag: fpr on nulls (should be near your nominal α), tpr on shortcut datasets, and tpr_delta_* by strength.

    embedding-mitigation¶

    Question. Can you train a task classifier that does not lean on the attribute?

    Data. Linear variant, 64 dimensions, task effect 0.8. Training: n = 2000 with A agreeing with Y at rho ∈ {0.9, 0.97}, attribute strength δ ∈ {1, 2}. Test: n = 4000 with rho = 0.5, so the attribute says nothing about the label. 4 settings × 5 seeds (quick: 1 seed).

    Entry. fit(embeddings, attribute, task_labels, seed) returning an object with predict(embeddings); optionally transform(embeddings).

    Metrics. worst_group_acc: test accuracy of the worst of the four (task, attribute) groups, averaged over settings (ranking metric); mean_acc over the groups; gain_over_erm, the worst-group gain over plain logistic regression on the same split; with transform, leakage_auroc, the out-of-fold AUROC of a probe predicting A from [transform(E), Y] on the test set (0.5 = nothing left).

    image-detection¶

    Question. Does a trained model rely on a hidden shortcut, and where is it?

    Data. 32×32 RGB images of one faint bar, horizontal or vertical (the task), on a noisy background. The shortcut is a 4×4 red patch in one corner; the corner is fixed within a dataset and chosen by its seed. The patch agrees with the label with probability rho ∈ {0.9, 0.95, 0.98, 1.0} (shortcut-trained) or 0.5 (null datasets: the patch is in half the images but carries no information). For each dataset the shared CNN is trained on 1500 images; the method gets 400 held-out images, their labels and the trained model, and no attribute labels. Seeds per strength: 9 nulls; 3, 2 and 2 at rho 0.9, 0.95 and 0.98; 6 at rho 1.0 (quick: 2 nulls, one each at 0.9 and 1.0).

    Ground truth is measured, not assumed. The training rho does not decide whether a given model actually uses the patch: at rho 0.9 one seed may lean on it and another not. So for every dataset the benchmark computes the model's reliance: the share of patched held-out images whose prediction changes when the patch is painted gray. A shortcut-trained dataset counts as a shortcut case when reliance ≥ 0.2, and as ambiguous otherwise. Below rho 1 the shared CNN usually learns the bar and leans on the patch only a little, so most clear shortcut cases come from rho 1; the partial strengths mostly test whether a score tracks how much the model relies on the patch. Null datasets are always nulls. Each dataset's rho, reliance and label are in details.per_dataset of results.json.

    Entry. detect(images, task_labels, model, seed) -> float | {"score", "flag", "heatmaps"} with images of shape (n, 3, 32, 32) in [0, 1] and heatmaps of shape (n, 32, 32).

    Metrics. auroc of the score, shortcut vs null datasets, ambiguous ones left out (ranking metric, after the flag groups); reliance_spearman: rank correlation between the score and the measured reliance over all datasets, ambiguous included (does the score track how much the model relies on the patch?); with heatmaps, pointing_game: share of patched images in shortcut datasets whose heatmap peak falls on the patch (within 2 pixels); with a flag, fpr on nulls and tpr on shortcut cases.

    image-mitigation¶

    Question. Can you train an image classifier that ignores the patch?

    Data. The same images. Training: n = 2000 with the patch agreeing with the label at rho ∈ {0.9, 0.97}. Test: n = 2000 with rho = 0.5. 2 settings × 3 seeds (quick: 1 seed). Trained without a usable patch (rho = 0.5) the shared CNN reached about 0.77 worst-group accuracy in our runs, a rough ceiling for this backbone and budget.

    Entry. fit(images, attribute, task_labels, seed) returning an object with predict(images).

    Metrics. worst_group_acc (ranking metric), mean_acc, gain_over_erm against the shared CNN trained with plain cross-entropy.

    Shared CNN¶

    shortcut_detect.leaderboard.image_models.make_cnn(): three 3×3 conv blocks (16, 32, 64 channels, BatchNorm, ReLU, max-pool after the first two), global average pooling and a linear head; train_classifier() trains it with Adam (lr 3e-3, batch 64, 8 epochs) on optionally weighted cross-entropy. Mitigation methods may use any model, but using this one keeps the comparison about the method.

    Reproducibility¶

    The committed scores are the ones computed on the CI runner (submitting). Embedding-detection scores reproduce exactly (verify tolerance 1e-6). Embedding-mitigation scores are checked with a tolerance of 0.01 and image-track scores with 0.02: entries that train with PyTorch can move slightly between CPU models (by about 0.002 for the contrastive encoder), even on the CI runners. Scores on fresh seeds: python -m shortcut_detect.leaderboard score contrib/<name> --seed-offset 1.
